:::::prose-p3
**Creating an online store** is a process that involves much more than choosing an e-commerce platform, preparing a graphic design and adding products. A professional implementation starts with an analysis of the business and customer needs, followed by strategy, and then covers UX/UI design, technology selection, development, integrations, testing and launching the store.

Decisions made at each stage have a direct impact on shopping convenience, conversion and performance, and thus on the final success and the future growth potential of the e-commerce store. But how exactly does the process of creating an online store look? In this article, we show step by step how to build an effective online store and what to pay attention to, so that the store is prepared for effective sales and further growth already at the planning stage.

## What are the steps in the process of creating an online store?

:::::prose-p3
The process of *<u><a href="/services/e-commerce/">creating an online store</a></u>* is worth dividing into several stages that allow you to build a solid and functional e-commerce platform. Their exact scope depends on the specifics of the store, the sales model and the required functionalities. A professional implementation most often includes:
:::::
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 1. Analysis of the business and the online store's requirements

:::::prose-p3
The first stage of creating an online store should be an analysis of business and functional needs. Before the design stage begins, it's worth determining what the store is going to sell, who the offer is aimed at, what the competition looks like, and which processes the store should support.

At this stage, it's worth establishing, among other things, the product range structure, the way prices and stock levels will be managed, payment and delivery methods, the required integrations and functionalities, as well as plans related to marketing strategy and sales growth.

A well-prepared analysis limits the risk of costly changes during development and allows the technology to be matched to the actual needs of the business. The choice between WooCommerce, PrestaShop or another technology should result from the needs of the specific e-commerce store, the required integrations and its future development plans, rather than solely from the platform's popularity or initial cost.
:::::
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 2. Choosing an e-commerce platform and a store development partner

:::::prose-p3
The next step after defining the business and functional requirements is choosing the e-commerce platform on which the store will be built. You don't have to make this decision on your own - an experienced development partner can recommend the right technology based on the project's assumptions, requirements, budget and further development plans.

Equally important is choosing the team that will design and implement the online store. Beyond their declared technical capabilities, it's worth checking their experience in building e-commerce stores, their portfolio of completed projects, and their approach to implementation quality and the store's future development.
:::::
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 3. Designing the store's UX/UI and architecture

:::::prose-p3
Once the analysis is complete and the technology has been chosen, the UX/UI design work for the online store begins. At this stage, the information architecture is created, along with a comprehensive plan for the layout of content, navigation, product categories and the purchase path. *<u><a href="/services/ux-ui-design/">UX design</a></u>* focuses on making it easy for the user to find the right product, move smoothly around the store, and go through the purchase process without unnecessary barriers.

Based on the prepared architecture and UX assumptions, the store's key views are designed, including the homepage, product listings, search and filtering, the product page, the cart, and checkout. Next, the UI layer is created, giving the store its final look while maintaining consistency with the brand's visual identity and properly showcasing the offer.
:::::
::::

::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 4. Development and implementation of the online store

:::::prose-p3
Once the UX/UI design is approved, implementation of the online store begins. During this stage, the graphic design is translated into a working e-commerce system, and developers implement the previously planned functionalities and the mechanisms responsible for the store's operation and sales process.

The quality of the development itself matters a great deal - the right architecture, optimal code and the way individual functionalities are implemented affect the store's performance, stability and the possibilities for further expansion. In the case of WooCommerce or PrestaShop, it's also worth limiting the excessive use of ready-made plugins and modules where dedicated development would be a better solution.
:::::
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5

## 5. Integrating the store with external systems

:::::prose-p3
The next stage is integrating the online store with external systems and services. Depending on the project's assumptions, these may include ERP, WMS and CRM systems, payment operators, courier companies, accounting software, marketing automation tools or marketplace platforms.

Integrations automate the flow of data between the store and other systems, streamlining day-to-day e-commerce operations and sales activities. They can be responsible, among other things, for synchronizing products, prices and stock levels, passing on orders and handling shipments, as well as automating marketing communication based on customer behavior.
:::::
::::

::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 6. Testing and optimizing the online store

:::::prose-p3
Once development is complete, it's time to thoroughly test the store. The key elements of the purchase path need to be verified above all - the product search and filtering, product pages, cart, order placement, payments, deliveries, and the operation of integrations with external systems. The store should also be checked on mobile devices, across various screen resolutions and popular browsers.

At this stage, it's also worth verifying the store's performance and its technical SEO elements, which affect its proper indexing and later ranking. Any errors and problems found should be eliminated before publication and the start of sales.

Before publication, it's also important to take care of the final store configuration, verify the content and product data, and connect analytics and marketing tools. This makes it possible to measure traffic and sales, and to run planned marketing activities, right from the moment the store goes live.
:::::
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 7. Publishing the store and launching sales

:::::prose-p3
Once testing is finished and the store is ready, it is published on the target domain and sales begin. After publication, it's worth re-verifying the most important elements of the purchase process to make sure everything works correctly in the production environment as well.

Publishing the store should go hand in hand with launching the planned marketing and sales activities - advertising campaigns, SEO efforts, content marketing or communication with existing customers. From this moment on, the store starts selling, acquiring customers and consistently building its position on the market.
:::::
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog mb-10 mt-10 [&_h2]:desktop:!text-2xl [&_li]:before:!bg-black [&_li]:!mb-1 [&_ul]:!mb-5
---

## 8. Administration and further development of the online store

:::::prose-p3
Once launched, the store requires ongoing administration and technical support. This includes, among other things, system updates, monitoring correct operation, and taking care of the store's security and stability.

At the same time, it's worth analyzing data on traffic, user behavior and sales, and then using it to develop the store further. Optimizing the purchase process, expanding functionalities, adding new integrations or improving UX allows the e-commerce store to be adapted to changing customer needs and a growing business scale.

An online store should not be treated as a closed project, but as a sales tool that should be continuously improved as the business grows.

## Which platforms for creating online stores are the best?

:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-0 [&_p]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-black
---

There is no single e-commerce platform that will be the best choice for every store. The decision should primarily result from the sales model, business assumptions, budget and further development plans. A simple store with a basic purchase process needs different capabilities than an e-commerce store with an extensive catalog, individual pricing policy or complex integrations.
::::

::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog [&_li]:!mb-2 [&_ul]:!mb-5 [&_h3]:desktop:!text-xl [&_h3]:!mb-2 [&_h3]:!mt-5
---

### **WooCommerce (WordPress)**
:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-0 [&_p]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-black
---

The most popular choice in Poland - ideal for small and medium-sized stores. A flexible solution based on WordPress that gives a lot of freedom in designing and expanding the store. WooCommerce allows you to build both standard online stores and more complex solutions with dedicated functionalities and integrations. A large plugin ecosystem makes it easier to extend the store's capabilities, but for very demanding projects, implementation quality and proper system architecture are essential.
::::


::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog [&_li]:!mb-2 [&_ul]:!mb-5 [&_h3]:desktop:!text-xl [&_h3]:!mb-2 [&_h3]:!mt-5
---

### **PrestaShop**
:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-0 [&_p]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-black
---

This is a platform built from the ground up with e-commerce in mind, and it works well for stores with a large and extensive product range. It offers wide-ranging options for managing the product catalog, variants, prices, promotions and orders, and thanks to its open code it can be extended with dedicated functionalities and integrations. Compared to WordPress, however, it offers less flexibility when it comes to creating extensive content, custom landing pages or content sections.
::::

::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog [&_li]:!mb-2 [&_ul]:!mb-5 [&_h3]:desktop:!text-xl [&_h3]:!mb-2 [&_h3]:!mt-5
---

### **Shoper**
:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-0 [&_p]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-black
---

Shoper is a Polish e-commerce platform operating on a SaaS model, which allows you to launch a store quickly without building your own technical infrastructure. Using it involves subscription fees, and the store itself runs on the provider's infrastructure - so this model can be compared to "renting" a store. In exchange, you get ready-made features and platform maintenance, but with less freedom to develop the store than with open-source solutions.
::::

::::effect-appear-md
---
class: mx-blog [&_li]:!mb-2 [&_ul]:!mb-5 [&_h3]:desktop:!text-xl [&_h3]:!mb-2 [&_h3]:!mt-5
---

### **Magento (Adobe Commerce)**
:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-0 [&_p]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-black
---

A sales platform intended primarily for advanced, large-scale e-commerce projects. It works well for stores with a large number of products, operating in multiple markets and language versions, requiring complex integrations and non-standard sales processes. It offers extensive configuration options and the ability to build dedicated features, but at the same time comes with a more complex implementation and maintenance process as well as higher technical and budget requirements.

## How to plan a budget for creating an online store?

:::::prose-p3
---
class: ml-0 [&_p]:!mb-[40px] [&_ul]:!mb-10 [&_li]:before:!bg-black
---

The cost of creating an online store depends primarily on the scope of the project, the chosen technology and the functionalities that need to be designed and implemented. When planning a budget, it's worth taking into account not only the cost of launching the store, but also its later maintenance and development.

**Platform and hosting** - costs depend on the chosen technology. For WooCommerce or PrestaShop, you need to factor in, among other things, hosting, the domain and any paid plugins and modules. SaaS platforms, such as Shoper, on the other hand, operate on a subscription model in which the infrastructure is part of the service.

**UX/UI design and implementation** - the cost depends on the complexity of the project, the scope of functionalities and the degree of customization of the store. Custom-built stores are more expensive to implement, but they offer greater flexibility and more possibilities for development and optimization than solutions based on ready-made templates. An individual design helps a brand stand out and build recognition, and dedicated features can streamline sales and create an advantage over the competition. It's important to plan the scope of such solutions in line with business needs and the established budget.

**Integrations** - connecting the store with ERP, WMS, an accounting system, marketplaces or other tools can significantly affect the scope and cost of implementation. What matters here is not only the number of integrations, but also their complexity and the way data is exchanged.

**Marketing and promotion** - launching a store is only the beginning of the road, which is why it's worth including funds for acquiring customers in the budget from the very start. Depending on the strategy, this may include ADS advertising campaigns, SEO, content marketing, social media or marketing automation. The size of this budget should result from the adopted strategy, the competitiveness of the market and the assumed sales goals.

**Store maintenance and development costs** - after the store is launched, it's worth including a budget for its ongoing technical administration, updates, bug fixes and infrastructure maintenance. A separate part of the budget should be set aside for further development - optimizing the purchase process, and adding new functionalities and integrations resulting from customer needs and a growing sales scale.

## What problems can occur when creating an online store?

:::::prose-p3
Creating an online store is a complex process, in which problems can arise at the planning, design and implementation stages alike. However, many of them can be limited through good analysis, proper project preparation and cooperation with an experienced development team.

**Scope changes during implementation** - imprecisely defined requirements can lead to further changes, longer implementation time and higher costs. That's why it's worth establishing the store's key functionalities and assumptions before design work begins.

**Integration problems** - connecting the store with ERP, WMS, marketplaces or other external systems can turn out to be more complex, especially when there are technical limitations on the side of the integrated systems or an unusual way of exchanging data.

**Technological limitations** - some functionalities may require a compromise between the original assumptions, technical possibilities and budget. In such situations, it's worth looking for alternative solutions that will allow you to achieve the intended business goal without unnecessarily increasing the project's complexity and cost.

**Data migration** - when building a new version of an existing store, transferring products, customers, orders and other data from the old system can be a challenge. Differences in data structure and a large number of records may require preparing dedicated migration mechanisms and thoroughly verifying the accuracy of the transferred information. That's why the scope and method of migration should be defined already at the project planning stage.

## What are the most common mistakes when creating an online store?

:::::prose-p3
Mistakes made at the planning and implementation stages of a store can later limit sales, hinder its development and generate additional costs. The most common ones include:

- **Insufficient market research** - not understanding customer needs and the market can lead to a store's failure. Analyzing the target audience and the competition makes it possible to better match the offer, functionalities and the way products are presented to the audience's expectations.
- **Lack of a well-thought-out strategy** - simply launching a store doesn't guarantee customers or sales. Even before launch, it's worth defining how the store will attract traffic, reach customers, and turn interest in the offer into sales.
- **Designing without taking UX and the purchase path into account** - an attractive appearance should go hand in hand with intuitive navigation, easy product search and as simple a checkout process as possible.
- **A repetitive store appearance** - using ready-made templates without adapting them to the brand can make the store look similar to the competition and fail to reflect its character. A custom design helps the store stand out, build brand recognition and be better remembered by customers.
- **Mismatching the implementation approach to the store's goal** - a different approach will work for a store built to test an idea, where ready-made templates and simpler solutions can be justified, and a different one for a project developed for the long term. In the latter case, it's worth taking care of implementation quality, flexibility and the possibility of further development from the very start, in order to avoid a costly rebuild as the business grows.

## What are the benefits of running an online store?

:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-10 [&_p]:!mb-6 [&_ul]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-transparent
---

An online store gives a company the opportunity to reach new customers, grow sales, and automate many processes. A well-designed e-commerce platform can become one of the key sales channels and an important element of business growth.

- **1. Greater Sales Reach** <br>
An online store allows you to reach customers regardless of their location and conduct sales around the clock. As your business grows, you can expand into new regions and international markets, increasing your sales volume without the limitations typical of brick-and-mortar stores.


- **2. Lower operating costs** <br>
The absence of costs associated with renting a storefront or maintaining a large staff means that the costs of running a business are lower. Many processes can be automated, which further reduces expenses.

- **3. Scaling sales** <br>
E-commerce allows you to expand your product range, increase the number of orders processed, and launch new sales channels. Properly implemented technology and integrations enable the store to grow alongside your business.

- **4. Access to data and analytics** <br>
An online store allows you to analyze, among other things, traffic sources, user behavior, product popularity, conversion rates, and points where customers abandon the purchasing process. This data helps you make decisions regarding marketing, product offerings, and further sales optimization.

- **5. Process automation** <br>
Integrations with ERP and WMS systems, payment gateways, shipping carriers, and marketing automation tools allow you to automate many repetitive processes. As a result, as the number of orders increases, not all areas require a proportional increase in team workload.

- **6. Full control over your own sales channel** <br>
Having your own store allows you to independently shape how products are presented, the purchasing process, brand communication, and sales activities. Unlike sales based solely on marketplaces, your company isn’t limited to a predefined way of presenting its offerings and can develop a direct relationship with customers.

## Summary

:::::prose-p3
---
class: mb-10 [&_p]:!mb-6 [&_ul]:!mb-5 [&_li]:before:!bg-transparent
---

Creating an online store gives a company the opportunity to build its own independent sales channel and reach customers without the limitations typical of brick-and-mortar retail. However, simply having a store does not guarantee success. Its effectiveness depends on a combination of a good business idea, the right strategy, a well-designed shopping experience, and high-quality execution.

It’s also important to view the store from the customer’s perspective—it should be easy to use, make it simple to find the right product and complete a purchase efficiently, while also setting the brand apart from the competition. From the company’s perspective, integration capabilities, process automation, data analysis, and the ability to adapt the store to changing business needs are equally important. Therefore, when planning an e-commerce investment, price should not be the sole factor determining the choice of solution and vendor. The most important thing is to create a solution tailored to the company’s goals, budget, and planned scale of operations—one that will effectively support sales both at launch and as the business continues to grow.
